# Globe interaction review

October 31, 2026. Branch: `feat/globe-interactions`, based on `24d1ac0`.

This work is for a future update. The submitted build 8, `v1.0.0` tag, App Store metadata and version/build settings are unchanged. Development builds are installed directly on the owner's RBDUO. No archive, upload or release was performed. Assign the next build number when preparing the eventual update.

## Behavior

- The cream, sage and pine palette, lighting, orthographic globe and starting composition are retained. At up to 400% zoom, the actual sphere extends beyond its original silhouette. Discover's canvas reaches the sides of its content area and gently fades into the page above and below, without a fixed circular crop.
- Country borders are spherical geometry with a thin width adjusted to the camera scale. They stay fine at close zoom instead of enlarging a raster stroke. Country fills and the subtle ocean grid retain the existing 4096 by 2048 texture; the change does not multiply texture memory.
- One finger uses independent screen-distance yaw and pitch, scaled to the current zoom. A horizontal drag cannot change latitude; a vertical drag cannot change longitude. Pitch stops continuously at 85 degrees in either direction, with immediate response on reversal. Starting near the silhouette does not change sensitivity. Camera scale and offset stay fixed, including an intentionally shifted close-up.
- A flick has a short 0.36-second glide with velocity decaying smoothly to zero. A pause before release suppresses the glide, and Reduce Motion disables it. Touching the globe stops navigation or inertia at the currently displayed orientation. Touch-count changes reset the velocity estimate and gesture snapshot.
- A pinch anchors the geography between the fingers, including when its midpoint moves. One gesture recognizer applies scale and translation from a shared touch snapshot, eliminating ordering differences between separate pinch and pan callbacks. Adding or lifting a finger starts a new snapshot without jumping.
- Two fingers also reposition the globe without changing zoom. Its center can leave the view. A reachable slice stays visible and the reset control remains available. One-finger drags in transparent corners scroll the page; a pinch may straddle the sphere when its midpoint is on the visible surface.
- Reset is absent initially, including from accessibility. The first actual touch on the visible sphere, including an ocean tap, reveals it for that screen session. Blank-page scrolling and initial country setup do not reveal it, and a fresh launch starts hidden again. Its overlay does not change layout. Reset smoothly coordinates zoom, position and orientation, centering the selected country. Reduce Motion makes reset and surprise navigation immediate.
- Tapping a country updates its marker and name without moving the globe or opening details. Tap the name to open discoveries. The existing journal visit behavior remains. Take Me Somewhere restores the framing and previews the new country first.
- The onboarding practice globe shares the gestures and still does not select or open countries. The marker retains its capped screen size at close zoom.

## Implementation

`WorldGlobeView.swift` owns the unified touch recognizer, SceneKit rendering, border strips and main-thread navigation animation. Border segments are subdivided along the sphere and their width is controlled by a small geometry shader. `GlobeInteraction.swift` contains testable surface projection, independent-axis screen dragging, time-based release velocity and glide, combined pinch transforms, recoverable framing bounds and reset interpolation. The one-finger path only assigns orientation; viewport mutation is confined to two-finger navigation and explicit navigation/reset. `RootView.swift` presents the name preview and reset control.

`GlobeTestGestures.h/.m` is compiled only into the UI test runner. XCTest has no public two-finger drag API, so this helper uses its event synthesis classes to deliver simultaneous touch paths. It is not linked into the shipping app and may need adaptation when Xcode changes. No runtime gesture hooks or test-only behavior were added to the app.

## Rotation diagnosis and design change

The base `24d1ac0` used incremental screen-distance quaternion turns with inertia. The early PR `b12ebb1` used independent latitude/longitude increments, preserving north-up orientation. The later `2564638` changed to an inverse surface solver and translated the camera to compensate when a point could not stay under the finger. That produced the earlier recorded offscreen drift. `56f55b9` removed the translation and bounded each solver step, but the owner rejected its abrupt rotation on RBDUO.

A new deterministic replay of the actual `dragging` function showed why: a purely horizontal 140-point sweep from latitude 32 degrees ended near 84 degrees at every tested sample rate. The inverse solver coupled horizontal motion to latitude to keep a grabbed geographic point under the finger. Limiting each step did not remove that pole pull or provide consistent control. The simulator reproduced the polar view and failed a geographic hit assertion after the same horizontal sweep. The older user recording only documents the earlier translation issue; it is not evidence of the latest physical-device complaint.

The inverse solver is now removed. Rotation again uses direct, independent screen-distance increments. The same replay retains latitude exactly at 32 degrees and the same final longitude for 15, 30, 60, 120 and 240 samples. No geographic point is forced to follow an impossible constrained surface path. Pinch anchoring remains exact and unchanged. Release motion is integrated analytically from a short timestamped velocity window, rather than adding frame-dependent increments. The final touch sample is applied before the glide starts. Regrabs and reset interruptions retain the displayed state without reconstructing or snapping orientation.

The previous 204,802 assertions bounded individual angular steps, kept north upright and checked framing, but did not prohibit a horizontal drag from turning toward a pole. They were insufficient evidence of acceptable motion. The replacement tests check axis independence, event-rate invariance, circle and reversal checkpoints, pole-limit reversal, release timing and interruption, in addition to rendered framing and country hits. Simulator motion review and device installation are reported separately from the owner's hands-on acceptance.
