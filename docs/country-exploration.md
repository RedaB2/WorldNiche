# Country exploration

Selecting a destination with Take me somewhere, tapping a country on the globe, or opening a country from Atlas does not mark it explored. One continuous clay progress line stays below the country toolbar and fills smoothly with the percentage of the card scrolled. The existing paper, pine, serif type and glass controls remain the visual language.

Progress is the furthest scroll offset divided by the available scroll distance, from zero at the top to 100 percent at the bottom. It updates during every scroll without section milestones or a reading pause. Scrolling back, reopening the card or loading more content never reduces saved progress. The bar follows fractional progress continuously; persistent writes are coalesced at each whole percentage and when scrolling stops or the card closes. Music links remain optional and do not require a subscription or external playback.

At the end, hold the leaf for 900 milliseconds, or pull up past the bottom and keep holding. A clay ring fills, changes to a pine check, and confirms that the country is saved to the journal. Releasing early cancels. VoiceOver and other assistive activation use the same completion action without a timed gesture. Reduce Motion removes the progress, ring and scale animations.

Scroll progress is a reading cue, not proof of comprehension. A fast scroll can reach 100 percent, but confirmation is still required to mark a country explored. Progress survives closing and relaunching on this device and stays within the existing guest/account workspaces. It is included in local data exports and erased with local data. Partial progress is not uploaded or shared with friends. Only confirmed completion uses the existing exploration sync and sharing preference. No backend change is required.

Previously explored countries remain explored. Reopening or refreshing them does not change their exploration dates. Bookmarks, private notes, feed retries and source attribution retain their existing behavior. Opening a country from Atlas dismisses search keyboard focus, so it does not obscure the journal controls when the sheet closes.

## Checking a country reading

The card opens with a short country history and a population figure with its year and source. The next card tells one story about a historic place, object or tradition. The app bundles the overview, population data and an initial set of facts. It can replace the fact catalog from WorldNiche’s published edition. Readers can open the sources from each section.

The first visit starts with the first reviewed fact. Reopening on the same local date resumes the current fact. Visiting on a later date, tapping Next fact, or pulling to refresh advances once through the available facts. Readers see the entire pool before it repeats. Ask Niche and the share sheet use the fact on screen. The local journal stores the seen IDs in the current guest or account workspace and includes them in local exports. Clearing device data erases them.

When reviewing a new reading or a correction, use these steps.

1. Open the country-specific source and match the place, community and time period to the claim. UNESCO’s descriptions of inscribed sites and practices, museum object records, national archives and published research are good starting points. A country homepage or search result cannot establish a detail about a particular object. [World Heritage List](https://data.unesco.org/explore/dataset/whc001/) and [Intangible Heritage List](https://data.unesco.org/explore/dataset/ich001/).
2. Write the title and body in your own words. Include a detail a reader can picture. Keep the source’s dates, uncertainty and local scope. For disputed origins or a claim of being first, check an independent specialist source and narrow the wording to what both accounts support.
3. For a fact change, update the country’s ISO entry in the website catalog and increment its revision. The first edition offers two facts per country. As maintainer, you can publish up to five per country or withdraw all facts from a country. Include the supporting passage and explain any conflicting accounts in the PR. [Catalog publication and verification](deployment.md#publishing-historical-fact-corrections).
4. For a new app build, maintain the overview, publisher and direct link in the country’s `introduction` in `countries.json`. Run `bash apps/ios/Scripts/test_readings.sh` to check the packaged readings and the app’s acceptance of published corrections. The reviewer checks the meaning of each changed claim against its source.

The launch edition contains 390 sourced facts, two distinct stories for every country. The limit of five leaves room for additions in a small offline catalog. A correction keeps the existing ID and a different story gets a new country-prefixed ID. Editors compare the proposed fact with the other entries for that country, check its historical claim and link directly to the supporting record. UNESCO site and practice records were matched to their country codes, including shared sites such as the stone circles of Senegal and The Gambia. Museum, archive and national records supply further stories such as the Palauan money bird depicted on a meeting-house beam and Papua New Guinea’s teenage flag designer. [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa Library](https://digital.library.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/show/31976) and [Papua New Guinea Department of Prime Minister and National Executive Council](https://www.pmnec.gov.pg/prime-minister-marape-honours-the-mothers-of-papua-new-guinea-and-the-women-behindthe-independence-struggle/).

Morocco’s card describes the traditional attribution of the Fez mosque to Fatima al-Fihri in the ninth century. Historical accounts differ about the founder, and treating the mosque as a modern university in its founding year would add a disputed claim. [IRCICA architectural inventory](https://www.islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/listings/mosque-and-university-karaouiyn), [Aga Khan Trust for Culture](https://the.akdn/en/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/akaa/rehabilitation-al-qaraouiyine-mosque) and [discussion of the medieval evidence](https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004427952/BP000004.pdf).

Each introduction covers one or two milestones. Where useful, distinguish the formation of an earlier kingdom, colonial rule, a declaration of independence and its recognition. The app links to each country’s specific historical record. Sources include national institutions, the US Office of the Historian and the public-domain historical background in the archived 2020 World Factbook. The globe, capital and region remain separate. [Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/countries) and [World Factbook archive](https://simonw.github.io/cia-world-factbook-2020/).

Population uses the UN’s medium projection for July 1, 2025. In the 2024 demographic indicators CSV, select `Time=2025` and `Variant=Medium`, match `ISO2_code` to the country, and multiply `TPopulation1July` (thousands) by 1,000. Keep the UN location ID so each link opens the underlying country and year. The app shows up to three significant digits and labels the year and projection. [UN World Population Prospects](https://population.un.org/wpp/) and [Morocco’s country and year record](https://data.un.org/Data.aspx?d=PopDiv&f=variableID%3A12%3BcrID%3A504%3BvarID%3A2%3BtimeID%3A76).

## Validation

`CountryReadingUITests` exercises Take me somewhere without premature exploration, shared progress between Discover and Atlas, gradual scroll progress, backward scrolling without regression, partial progress across relaunch, cancelling a short hold, completing and reopening, bottom pull and hold, unavailable feeds and accessibility text sizes. The globe selection test separately checks that previewing countries leaves their discovery incomplete and does not increase explored history. Reading tests use the real bundled text and deterministic music responses. Coverage includes dated population, fact rotation across relaunch, the matching Ask Niche source and the local radio chart credit. Journal checks cover scroll geometry, continuous fractions, monotonic persistence, legacy decoding, sync isolation, confirmation, duplicate completion and workspace reset/restore.

Simulator results and the review video are kept under ignored `outputs/country-reading-progress/`. The owner's authorized TestFlight candidate is **1.0.1 (13)**; package and availability evidence are recorded in [TestFlight status](testflight.md) and ignored `outputs/testflight/1.0.1-13/`. App Store and backend releases remain separate.
